A Beginner's Guide to Writing Better AI Prompts

Aug 20, 2026 6 min read By the Bhailaana team

If you've ever typed a question into an AI chatbot and gotten a vague, generic answer back, the problem usually isn't the AI — it's the prompt. The good news: writing a better prompt is a skill anyone can learn in about ten minutes.

1. Say what you want, not just what you're thinking about

Compare these two prompts:

"Tell me about marketing."
"Give me 5 low-budget marketing ideas for a small pet-supplies store trying to reach first-time cat owners."

The second one gives the AI a role, a constraint (low budget), and an audience (first-time cat owners). That's the difference between a generic essay and something you can actually use.

2. Give it context it can't guess

AI doesn't know your business, your audience, or your goals unless you tell it. A simple structure that works well:

  • Who you are: "I run a small online pet store."
  • What you need: "I need 3 Instagram caption ideas."
  • Any constraints: "Keep them under 150 characters and friendly in tone."

3. Ask it to show its work

For anything involving reasoning — comparing options, planning steps, debugging — add a simple instruction like "explain your reasoning before giving the final answer". This alone noticeably improves accuracy on harder questions.

4. Iterate instead of starting over

Your first prompt rarely needs to be perfect. Treat it like a conversation: if the answer is close but not quite right, say exactly what to change — "make this shorter," "more casual tone," "add an example." This is faster than rewriting the whole prompt from scratch.

5. A simple template to reuse

Role: [who the AI should act as]
Task: [exactly what you want]
Context: [anything it needs to know]
Format: [how you want the answer structured]

Try this template on your next few prompts and you'll notice the difference immediately — sharper, more relevant answers with far less back-and-forth.

✏️ See It In Action

Here's the template from above, filled in for a real request — asking AI to write a product description for an online pet store:

Role: You are an experienced e-commerce copywriter who writes concise, benefit-focused product descriptions.

Task: Write a 60-word product description for a stainless steel automatic pet feeder with a companion app.

Context: My customers are busy pet owners aged 25–45 who care about their pet's health but don't have time to hand-feed on a strict schedule. Keep the tone warm and reassuring, not overly salesy.

Format: One short paragraph, followed by 3 bullet points highlighting key features.

Notice every line answers one specific question — who the AI is, what to make, why it matters to the reader, and how to shape the result. That's the whole trick: a vague ask becomes an easy one once you've answered those four questions yourself.
